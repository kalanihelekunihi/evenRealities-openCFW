## Arithmetic and simulator quirks requiring directed probes

- **Complex accumulate extreme:** `op_dspv2.c:1565–1595` gives mulca/mulcax an explicit x=y=80008000 special case returning7fffffff. The accumulate helpers at1597–1620 form the dual product sum in int32_t without that special case, then call saturated add. QEMU’s `meson.build:299–306` explicitly uses-fwrapv. Consequently for x=y=80008000,z=0 the helper source predicts80000000 for mulaca/mulacax, while mulca/mulcax return7fffffff. This is a concrete code-level difference worth an opcode microprobe and authoritative ISA cross-check, **not a confirmed hardware bug**. Stock tables may exclude that exact operand pair from a reachable FFT multiply. Record reachability separately from generic opcode validation. Do not silently patch QEMU or declare target equivalence from results on a suspect backend.
- **Saturation and lane order:** packed add/sub/cross helpers saturate each signed16 lane; pabs/pneg map8000 to7fff, and abs-max uses unsigned pmax.u16. Probe unequal high/low lanes, positive/negative overflow and the minimum-negative case. `pasxh_s16`’s explanatory comment does not accurately spell its exchanged operands; inspect implementation/ISA rather than using comments as the oracle.
- **Halving is arithmetic shift:** paddh/psubh/pasxh/psaxh implementations widen then arithmetic-shift by1. Negative odd sums/differences distinguish floor-style signed shift from truncation toward zero. The used9-section inventory contains no rounded-shift `.r` variant; do not apply nearest rounding/tolerance to explain away differences.
- **Shift count domains:** pasr.s16 explicitly masks the count to5 bits in `translate_v2.c:8940–8954`. Saturating plsl.s16.s helpers accept the passed count and handle values above16, despite comments mentioning low4 bits. The planned ±15 shifts avoid this ambiguity. Additional16/31/32 tests are backend/ISA diagnostics, not an expansion of a verified portable C contract; negative signed left shifts and large shifts in the authentic C source are not universal language-level equivalence evidence.
- **Loop semantics:** `translate_v2.c:4181–4188` models bloop as decrement then nonzero backward branch; it does not model target loop-cache timing. Probe counts1/2, source-prechecked0, odd tails and nested outer/inner FFT iterations, and verify the actual encoded backward target. A standalone unguarded zero-count bloop wraps the counter; the canonical bitreversal loop expects a valid positive table count. Do not infer timing or interrupt behavior from this numerical model.
- **Memory alignment/postincrement:** pldbi.d is two32-bit loads plus two4-byte pointer updates (`translate_v2.c:2488–2504`); variable ldbir snapshots its step register before access. These DSP memory routes use MO_TEUL rather than an explicit MO_ALIGN flag. QEMU unaligned-access behavior therefore does not by itself establish physical CK804 alignment acceptance. Keep buffers/tables4-byte aligned and probe pointer updates, reverse steps, register-alias cases actually used by the code, and guarded bounds. Do not introduce misaligned “equivalence” vectors until an ISA/ABI contract exists.

C and stock assembly can share simulator operations, so equal outputs may share a backend error.
