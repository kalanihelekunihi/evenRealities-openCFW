Instructions execute one-at-a-time to avoid the already recorded continuous-Unicorn IT-state anomaly; no stock byte/flag/helper-result substitutions.

Successful mutex take decrements availability, publishes current holder and increments held count once. Recursive reentry increments depth without taking another underlying mutex; final recursive give releases underlying ownership, clears holder and decrements held count. Nonowner recursive give returns failure. Normal CMSIS release does not pre-check ownership: a wrong owner reaches kernel assertions rather than a promised error status. Constructed recursion/held-count wrap probes are malformed-state arithmetic evidence, not live valid-use cases.

A blocked requester inherits priority into its holder and joins receive/delayed or suspended lists before the actual port-yield boundary. Finite, indefinite and tick-wrap waits are tested. Independent ownership/count/priority/container invariants accompany original/native comparisons. Three invalid-input paths stop before assertion fault sequences. Synthetic tick advancement at timeout-check entry exercises expiration **before first block**, with inheritance not yet established. The full source contains post-handover timeout disinherit, but that continuation is not dynamically validated. The private highest-waiter expression and disinherit provider have direct evidence separately; do not turn that into a completed resumed transaction claim.

CMSIS handle bit0 selects recursion, remaining bits the queue address. IRQ-style acquire/release returns-6, invalid decoded handle-4, unavailable no-wait acquire-3 and positive-wait failure mapping-2. Wait units are scheduler ticks, not verified milliseconds. Physical scheduling, task handover, exception return, hardware lifetime fault and firmware patch safety are unverified.
