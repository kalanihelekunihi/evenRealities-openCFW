0x58FAD2 captures zero-extended32-bit OS tick as64bits. It sign-extends the supplied32-bit timeout, adds it to the initial64-bit tick, and compares unsigned64-bit deadline against current zero-extended tick **before attempting a byte read**. Deadline reached returnsFFFFFFFF; a one-byte drain returns1; failed read delays1 OS tick and rereads time. Timeout0 therefore rejects even a byte already staged. Exact deadline arrival is rejected before drain, leaving staged data for a later call.

This differs from normal modular32-bit elapsed-time arithmetic. With initialFFFFFFFE and timeout5, deadline100000003 exceeds every zero-extended32-bit current tick. A wrapped synthetic tick cannot reach it; absent data remains at the eight-delay cut. With timeout1 the deadlineFFFFFFFF is representable and reached before wrap. For large values with sign bit set, signed extension can cause an earlier deadline or unsigned64-bit underflow, depending start. These are tested arithmetic/invalid-input fixtures, **not measured hangs or supported negative-timeout API recommendations**.

The actual tick wrapper explicitly clearsR1 after kernel read; fixtures deliberately supply a nonzero scratchR1 to verify zero-extension. OSdelay1 remains a boundary with synthetic advancing ticks, not actual task scheduling. Units are OS ticks; no millisecond conversion is asserted. Arrival0/1/3/noarrival, initial0/100/FFFFFFFE/FFFFFFFF, and timeouts0/1/2/5/7FFFFFFF/80000000/FFFFFFFF are tested. Cut cases prove finite observed behavior, not an infinite execution.

UART3 role remains GX8002 codec control/DFU. This one-byte serial helper drains its63usable staging ring; it is not the temple-sync24KiBstream. Oldest-byte eviction in the ring and rejected-suffix stream admission remain separate controlled-pressure behaviors. Codec response parser0x57C1FC uses a different elapsed-time subtraction path; its CRC/framing/deadline composition remains a concrete lead and must not inherit this helper's arithmetic without testing.
